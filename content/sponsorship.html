Common employer-sponsored pathways

Which sponsorship pathways should you know?

Subclass numbers identify a visa, but do not determine whether it suits a particular case. The role, employer and professional’s circumstances all matter.

SKILLS IN DEMAND · SUBCLASS 482

Temporary skilled work with an Australian employer.

The Skills in Demand visa allows an approved employer to sponsor a suitably skilled professional for a nominated role. It is a temporary work visa, and the employer, position and applicant’s background are assessed as part of the process.

What the assessment considers

  • The employer’s business, sponsorship status and obligations.
  • Whether the nominated role and its terms align with the applicable requirements.
  • The applicant’s relevant skills, qualifications, work history and supporting evidence.

How we can support

We can help coordinate the employer and professional sides of the matter—from role and candidate context through pathway assessment, information gathering and application preparation. A 482 matter may also sit within a longer-term plan, which should be considered against the individual facts and current rules.

EMPLOYER NOMINATION SCHEME · SUBCLASS 186

Permanent employer-nominated pathways.

The Employer Nomination Scheme is a permanent visa pathway involving an Australian employer’s nomination. The relevant stream and requirements depend on the applicant’s background, the role and the employer’s circumstances.

Two commonly discussed streams

  • Direct Entry may be relevant for a professional seeking a permanent employer-nominated pathway without relying on a prior 482-to-186 transition. Specific criteria and exemptions need individual review.
  • Temporary Residence Transition may be relevant to some professionals with eligible sponsored work history. The qualifying employment and other criteria must be checked against current rules.

How we can support

We can help examine the nomination context, role, professional history and available evidence, and coordinate the preparation across employer and applicant. Where a matter has complications or a less straightforward history, we can assess the issues before mapping possible next steps.

Visa names, criteria and processes can change. The summaries above are general information, not individual migration advice. Check the current Department of Home Affairs information or speak with a qualified professional about your circumstances.
