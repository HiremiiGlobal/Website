BUSINESS CASES

Roles, nominations and workplace preparation shaped around the business.

FINTECH · VIC

Fintech: 186 DE nomination for a Software Engineer

186 · Direct Entry nomination

The situation

A digital payments business needed a new software engineering role to support its product development and regulated operations.

What the team prepared

The nomination preparation connected the role with actual business projects, set out the engineering duties and brought together financial, employment and business records. Salary evidence was supported by independent market information and more than 20 relevant job advertisements.

What mattered

The key was explaining why the business needed the role and showing how the supporting records fitted that explanation.

Project outcome

Nomination approved without a further information request.

This is an employer nomination outcome, not a visa grant.

FINANCIAL SERVICES · VIC

Financial services: 186 DE nomination for a technology leadership role

186 · Direct Entry nomination

The situation

A financial services business was expanding its internal IT capability as part of a digital transformation programme.

What the team prepared

The team linked the proposed role to platform integration, cybersecurity and the employer’s operating plans. Preparation included market salary research, employment classification review and organised labour market testing records.

What mattered

Business context and technical duties needed to tell the same story, rather than relying on a generic position description.

Project outcome

Nomination approved without a further information request.

This is an employer nomination outcome, not a visa grant.

HEALTHCARE · QLD

Healthcare: recruitment and onboarding across multiple locations

HR & workforce support

The situation

A healthcare and training business needed to coordinate recruitment, onboarding and employment records across several locations.

What the team prepared

The engagement brought workforce planning, role classifications, job advertising templates and onboarding records into a shared framework, alongside preparation for potential 482 and 186 matters.

What mattered

Preparing the employment records early can make later workforce and sponsorship work easier to coordinate.

Project outcome

Shared HR processes and reusable recruitment records, with centralised tracking and reporting.

CROSS-BORDER BUSINESS · NSW

Cross-border business: HR and payroll coordination in Australia

HR systems & integration

The situation

The Australian operations of an overseas business needed a more consistent approach to local hiring, payroll and workforce coordination.

What the team prepared

The work linked workforce planning, recruitment, onboarding and payroll records, clarified communication with overseas management and prepared templates for future sponsorship and 482 nomination matters.

What mattered

A cross-border business needs local records and responsibilities to be clear, not just aligned with overseas group processes.

Project outcome

A unified framework for HR, recruitment and payroll coordination across the Australian operations.

INDIVIDUAL CASES

Connecting an applicant’s qualifications and experience with the proposed work.

RESOURCES · WA

Resources: 186 DE preparation for a Materials Engineer

186 · Direct Entry

The situation

A materials engineering case in Western Australia’s resources sector, involving an applicant’s technical background and a proposed employer-nominated role.

What the team prepared

The preparation focused on connecting the applicant’s technical experience with the nominated duties and employer information. The support also covered skills assessment requirements and sponsorship compliance, so the applicant-side records and employer-side information could be considered together.

What mattered

A job title alone does not explain the fit. The technical work behind it, the applicant’s experience and the employer’s need all matter to the preparation.

Support focus

Employer nomination, skills assessment preparation and the connection between technical experience and the proposed role.

CONSTRUCTION · WA

Construction: 482 sponsorship preparation for a Civil Engineering Draftsperson

482 · Skills in Demand

The situation

A civil engineering drafting case in Western Australia’s construction sector, with preparation centred on occupation criteria and the duties of the proposed position.

What the team prepared

The work focused on reviewing what the role involved beyond its title, organising the position information and identifying the records supporting those duties. Employer sponsorship guidance connected the occupation criteria with the information provided for the role.

What mattered

A useful position description explains the actual work. Clear duties and supporting information provide a stronger basis for reviewing the role than the title on its own.

Support focus

Occupation criteria, the detail behind the proposed duties and practical employer sponsorship preparation.

FINANCIAL SERVICES · VIC

Financial services: 482 preparation for an Investment Financial Adviser

482 · Skills in Demand

The situation

An investment financial adviser case in Victoria, involving the relationship between the applicant’s qualifications, professional experience and proposed position.

What the team prepared

The support covered qualifications review, refinement of the role information and structured case preparation. The focus was on showing how the applicant’s education and professional experience related to the position, rather than treating each part as a separate set of documents.

What mattered

Qualifications and experience are most useful when their relevance to the proposed work is clear. Preparation should make those connections easy to follow.

Support focus

Qualifications review, role refinement and a coherent presentation of the applicant’s professional background.
